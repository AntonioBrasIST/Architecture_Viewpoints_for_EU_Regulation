# DORA Back-end Delivery View Explanation

## 1. Executive overview and stakeholder scope

### View 0 — stakeholder-aware regulation map

The immutable DORA overview is retained in `0_dora_regulation_overview.archimate` and its reproducible pyArchimate source. It contains the approved legal core and the role path from Employer Bank to Back-end Developer, while preserving that the regulated entity is the Employer Bank as a credit institution, not the individual developer. The overview and this document make no compliance claim.

### Role and operating boundary

The stakeholder is an execution-level Back-end Developer. The approved footprint establishes independent implementation, testing, and maintenance of C# platform services, while work allocation, architecture, requirements, approvals, and go-live decisions remain with the Team Manager, Solution Architect, and Board.

The view boundary covers C# platform development and maintenance; offloads, query services, and ETLs; test/DEV readiness; service updates; production monitoring; incident and remediation interfaces; enterprise-data-platform dependencies; Azure AD/internal access constraints; and the employer's Microsoft contract. Consuming teams own the data. Contract management, provider oversight, incident classification ownership, and enterprise governance are not attributed to the developer.

### DORA mandate summary

- Articles 7–8: ICT systems, asset and dependency governance, inventories, and risk information.
- Article 9: protection, access, data transfer, security controls, and controlled change.
- Articles 10–13 and 17: monitoring, continuity, backup, recovery, learning, and incident handling.
- Articles 24–25: resilience testing and technical testing methods.
- Article 28: Microsoft cloud-provider risk, contractual controls, audit, termination, and exit planning.

### Coverage statement

All approved in-scope requirements are represented once by `COV-001` through `COV-153` and their approved regulatory-effect relationships. The remaining approved operational evidence is retained in the Role Context stream (`CTX-001` through `CTX-025`), including specification/mainframe dependencies, tooling, provisioning, work allocation, decision intake, and documented visibility limits. The approved uncovered register is empty.

The eight recorded concerns are treated as evidence-backed delivery constraints: external dependencies, permissions, specification quality, infrastructure availability, platform outage impact, limited cross-project visibility, and approval prerequisites. In particular, `CON-007` is a potential operational visibility limitation from the developer’s perspective, not evidence of enterprise non-compliance.

## 3. Layer-by-layer architectural walkthrough

### Motivation layer

The Motivation layer contains 13 parent Requirement families and 153 child Requirements. Every child is visually nested in its parent and semantically composed through `REL-COMP-S001–153`. Requirement titles are short reader-facing labels; the full atomic duty and readable DORA citation are retained in the traceability table. There are no Article boxes or hidden catalogue nodes.

### Business layer

The stakeholder is represented as the Back-end Developer business role. Employer Bank, Monitoring Team, Responsible Dev Team (ledger endpoint: Responsible Development Team), Infrastructure Team, and Technical Reviewer are actors or roles where the approved evidence assigns a behavior. The behavior path includes platform development, framework maintenance, service update, test and DEV readiness, monitoring, escalation, Software Remediation, the external Infra Remediation process (ledger endpoint: Infrastructure Remediation), pull-request creation, and pull-request review. Datadog Alarm, Software Error, and Infrastructure Problem are events; Pull Request and Software Fix are business objects.

### Application layer

Internal Framework, Offload Service, Enterprise Data Platform, Datadog, and Azure AD Access Control are application components in this viewpoint. They appear only where the approved relationship ledger connects them to stakeholder work or a regulatory effect. Azure AD Access Control is documented with its canonical ledger endpoint name, Azure AD / Internal Service Access.

### Technology and implementation layers

The approved stakeholder graph contains no Technology-layer node or Implementation-and-Migration element. Infrastructure provisioning and recovery execution are represented only through approved external operational relationships, not through invented hosting, deployment, or work-package elements.
